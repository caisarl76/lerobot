Offline audit · Unitree G1 + Dex3 · SONIC v1.1 · 2026-09-23

Do SONIC tokens bring the hand back to where the dataset put it?

For the arms, yes: encode → token → decode lands the palm within 3–4.5 cm (p95) of the trajectory the tokens were built from. What misses the 5 cm gate is the converter's speed limiter, which slows fast arm motions before encoding. It is SONIC-independent and fixable.

Update: no-speed-limit datasets built and audited

Chosen path: no arm/hand speed limit in the converter, train on 30 Hz, and at deploy linearly interpolate the policy's 30 Hz tokens to 50 Hz. In the official deploy, interpolation matched tokens encoded at 50 Hz to within 0.1 cm, so a 50 Hz dataset is not needed. Datasets: /run-output/datasets/sonic78_nolimit (3152 ep) and /run-output/humanoid_everyday_g1_20260923/datasets/sonic78_nolimit (4064 ep). Both are published, counts match the source, and 0 values were rate-limited; the stored tokens for the six replay-tested episodes are bit-identical to what ran through the deploy.

The value depends on the episode sample (4.59 cm on an 83-episode sample), so treat it as about 4.6–5.2 cm. For the 50 Hz token feed, only look-ahead interpolation helps (HE p95 4.72 → 4.59 cm vs. holding); causal interpolation inside the controller is worse (5.08 cm).

Deploy streaming (2026-09-24): runtime is NVIDIA's C++ g1_deploy_onnx_ref fed over ZMQ. In the official deploy, a VLA-like chunked stream (40-token chunks every 0.4 s, ChunkResampler look-ahead to 50 Hz) matched ideal look-ahead within 0.02 cm on all six replay episodes (median palm p95 4.05 cm vs 4.49 cm when holding tokens), with unchanged stability.

What was measured

28D / 43D joint action→ clip + 1 rad/s arm slew (converter)→ v1.1 encoder→ 64D token @ source fps→ v1.1 decoder @ 50 Hz, MuJoCo closed loop→ palm point via FK

Stored tokens are read from the converted parquet and held at 50 Hz between source frames. The decoder runs closed loop against the official scene_43dof.xml plant at 200 Hz with the policy_parameters.hpp gains. The history resets each episode and a 1 s standing warm-up runs first. The palm point is the mean of the index_0 and middle_0 link origins, so it does not depend on finger angles. Gate: p95 reached-palm error vs. the original dataset command > 5 cm ⇒ fail, measured in the pelvis frame.

  • The decoder's output is a PD setpoint, not a joint pose. With the official soft gains (arm kp ≈ 14 Nm/rad), the policy commands 0.2–0.9 rad past the target to fight gravity. Comparing decoded actions directly against dataset joints is invalid; the pose the robot actually reaches is the right comparison.
  • Lag: the best cross-correlation shift is 2 source frames (≈67 ms) for Unitree and 3 for HE. At the best lag, the mean palm error drops to 1.7 cm (Unitree) and 2.6 cm (HE).
  • Clipping is harmless. All clipped values are Dex3 finger joints exceeding the limit by ≤ 0.001 rad. Arms are never clipped.
  • The speed limiter is the cause. 2–7% of HE arm frames exceed 1 rad/s. Re-running the 12 worst episodes without the limiter: HE p95 falls from 15–24 cm to 4.07 cm and Unitree from 6.7–12.2 cm to 5.49 cm, with no falls.

  • Unitree has no drift. The SONIC-only error slope has a median of 0.004 cm per 10 s, and the first and last quarters of episodes are equal (1.92 vs 1.97 cm).
  • Errors arrive in short bursts at fast motions. Unitree has 96 bursts over 5 cm, with a median length of 0.33 s. 92% start within 1 s of a source arm motion faster than 1 rad/s, 76% are mostly speed-limiter error, and 82% fall back below 2 cm within 2 s.
  • SONIC's own error barely depends on speed: p95 is 3.45 cm at < 0.5 rad/s and 3.96 cm at 2–3 rad/s. The limiter's error climbs from 0.1 to 10.5 cm across the same range.
  • HE error does grow late in episodes, and that growth is the limiter. During long, fast motions the limited trajectory falls behind and never catches up before the episode ends. The SONIC-only part drifts slightly, from 2.5 to 3.4 cm over 40 s.
  • HE has real single-frame pose jumps: 119 frames faster than 6 rad/s in 75 episodes, up to 30 rad/s (≈1 rad in one frame). Unitree has 4.

Sample: 2 episodes per task (up to 15 tasks) plus the most-clipped and most speed-limited, run both ways (Unitree 32 pairs, HE 30 pairs). In sim, removing the arm limiter costs nothing in balance or torque, and the robot still never moves an arm faster than 4.9 rad/s: the decoder and the 10 Hz PD smooth out even 30 rad/s source jumps.

Dex3 fingers never go through SONIC

The 14 hand channels are copied into the 78D action after a 2 rad/s speed limit. Stored vs. source: p95 0.003 rad (Unitree) and 0.098 rad (HE), but up to 1.61 rad during fast grasps. A trained VLA would learn grasps that close later than the teleoperator commanded. The token check cannot validate the hands; this pass-through check is the only evidence for them.

Decision needed

  • Unitree sonic78 is usable on this criterion, but it bakes the 1 rad/s arm / 2 rad/s hand limiter into the training targets.
  • Humanoid Everyday fails under the current converter. Either raise or remove the limiter (the ablation passes at 4.07 cm), or accept the slowed targets and gate against the limited reference instead (4.48 cm).
  • The limiter copies the reference VLA adapter's setting. A token-output VLA has no joint-space limiter at runtime (no encoder runs on the robot), so the training data is the only place this cap would act.
  • Recommendation: drop the 1 rad/s arm cap, but keep a guard against source glitches: a ~5 rad/s cap, or drop/repair episodes with single-frame jumps. Evidence is sim-only; the hand limiter is a separate decision this audit cannot test.

Limits of this evidence

  • Plant is MuJoCo sim2sim, not the real robot. Dex3 fingers are unactuated in sim, and the scene's hand model is flagged unstable by NVIDIA.
  • 28D data has no legs or root: tokens encode nominal standing legs and identity heading. No locomotion target is validated.
  • Calibration: a constant standing pose tracks at 1.04 cm p95. The dance example was invalid (identity heading on a turning motion) and was excluded.
  • Sampling: 4 random episodes per task (≤ 20 tasks) plus the 4 most-clipped and 4 most speed-limited from the converter reports; seed 0.
  • HE numbers come from a dataset still being written; re-run after prepare-sonic78.exit = 0.
  • Sim XML and converter XML have identical body joints (ranges, axes, offsets). They differ only in Dex3 geometry, which shifts the palm point by a constant 2.2 cm on both sides of the comparison.
